A programmable alphabet for aromatic materials.

Grapheins is building a new class of sequence-defined aromatic materials — chains of fused-aromatic building blocks joined, unit by unit, by the same amide bond that assembles every protein in biology.

The idea

Biology builds with sequence. Chemistry mostly builds at random.

Proteins owe their precision to one thing: a small alphabet of building blocks arranged in a defined sequence, joined head-to-tail by amide bonds, folding into exact shapes. Most synthetic polymers, by contrast, are assembled randomly or by simple repetition.

Grapheins closes that gap from an unusual direction. Our building block is not a biological amino acid but a fused-aromatic core — in the first generation, a well-characterized polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon used as a manageable, low-cost surrogate for a fragment of nanographene. Each core carries two directional handles, one amino and one carboxyl, so the blocks couple head-to-tail through ordinary amide bonds, exactly as peptides do.

The result is a sequence-defined chain whose backbone presents flat, π-rich aromatic faces instead of biological side chains — a molecular Lego in which the identity and the order of every unit is programmed.

01

Molecular biomimicry

We treat a nanographene surrogate as if it were an amino acid, with defined N-to-C polarity — so decades of peptide-coupling chemistry become directly usable.

02

Sequence definition

Chains are built one unit at a time, in a programmed order. The sequence is the design variable — a vast, addressable space of π-conjugated materials.

03

Computation-to-lab selection

An in-silico screen ranks candidate sequences so we synthesize only the few worth making — spending simulation cycles instead of reagents.

Where we are — stated plainly

A complete design and preliminary simulations. Function still to be proven.

We hold ourselves to the same filter we apply to any claim: a molecule is worth nothing until it is synthesized, isolated, and its structure characterized beyond doubt. As of today, Grapheins has a complete molecular design and preliminary computational support. We do not claim any demonstrated functional property. Our next step is a focused proof of concept — to synthesize, purify and characterize a single main-chain amide bond between two aromatic building blocks, confirmed independently.
